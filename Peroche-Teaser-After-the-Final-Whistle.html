The Peroche View

The window has a closing date.

Two clocks are running, and both run out in the third quarter. The first is a refinancing wall: roughly USD 48 billion in hotel loan maturities come due through 2026, with non-rated hotel debt now priced at SOFR plus 525 basis points — a 375 basis point premium over investment grade, the widest spread since 2009. That is not a statistic. It is a public schedule of owners who must sell or refinance into a market that has turned against them.

The second clock is absorption. Latin America's hotel pipeline is at a multi-year high, and the best-prepared capital is already underwriting the entries. The real cost of watching US host cities through July is not a quiet summer. It is arriving at the Latin American window a quarter late, behind the capital that read the board early.

Where the Capital Is Moving

Capital is not leaving hospitality. It is relocating.

In the United States, transactions are concentrating in the most liquid gateways, where quality assets trade below replacement cost. New York alone cleared USD 3.69 billion in hotel sales last year, up 87% — the deepest hotel market in the country.

Outside the US, the move is structural, not opportunistic: 755 projects and 113,663 rooms in the Latin American pipeline, with Mexico holding a third of it and luxury supply at a record. These are the two ends of a single trade.
