Section 01  ·  Executive Summary

Investment thesis: the boom that missed, and the window it opened

The 2026 FIFA World Cup was sold to the hospitality industry as a once-in-a-generation demand event. Capital positioned accordingly. Six weeks before kickoff, that bet is not paying out. Roughly four in five US hoteliers report bookings tracking below forecast, and FIFA has clawed back room blocks across all sixteen host cities. The demand signal that drove a wave of positioning turned out to be, in part, an artifact of those blocks.

While attention concentrated on US host cities, two forces have been quietly repricing the hospitality map of the Americas. The first is a refinancing wall: a concentrated wave of hotel loan maturities forcing US owners into extensions, distressed refinancings, and motivated sales. The second is a structural reallocation of capital toward Latin America, where the construction pipeline sits at a multi-year high and Mexico alone accounts for a third of the regional pipeline.

The durable, mispriced opportunity is not the event. It is the window the event opens after the final whistle.

Section 02  ·  The Setup

Why the obvious bet missed

2.1 — The non-event

Three mechanisms broke the link between tournament scale and hotel demand. First, the early demand signal was partly manufactured — FIFA reserved large room blocks that registered as organic demand in forecasting models, then exercised an opt-out clause, cancelling blocks as high as 70% in Boston, Dallas, Los Angeles, Philadelphia, and Seattle. Second, international demand is throttled at the border: ~65% of hoteliers cite visa-processing delays as an active suppressant. Third, the spike is narrow and perishable by design — even where match-day demand materializes, it evaporates the day after the final.

2.2 — The refinancing wall

Roughly USD 48 billion in CMBS hotel-loan maturities run through 2025–2026, with ~30% of hotel and motel loans maturing in 2026 after 35% matured in 2025. These loans were sized in a lower-rate world and now come due against compressed margins and selective lenders. Investment-grade platforms refinance near SOFR+150 bps. Non-rated hotel portfolios face SOFR+525 bps — a 375 bps spread, the widest since 2009. Owners who counted on a World Cup revenue bridge to refinance into are discovering the bridge is shorter and weaker than modeled.

Distress does not destroy good assets; it transfers them. The refinancing wall is a schedule of motivated sellers, and that schedule is public.

2.3 — The pivot

Capital is reading the same data, and it is not leaving the asset class. In the US, transaction activity is concentrating in liquid gateway markets where buyers can acquire below replacement cost. NYC recorded USD 3.69 billion of hotel transactions in 2025, up 87% — the most liquid hotel market in the country. Outside the US, the pivot is structural: LatAm's pipeline sits at 755 projects and 113,663 rooms as of Q1 2026, up 6% YoY in projects, with luxury supply at 142 projects and 26,590 rooms.

Section 05  ·  Cross-Cutting Frameworks

Five disciplines that govern execution across every market

01
Underwriting past the whistle
Strip the tournament months out. Underwrite trailing structural ADR and occupancy as base case. Treat the event as upside with its own probability. Stress the international segment for visa and geopolitical friction. Demand that survives this test is worth capitalizing.
02
Capital-stack and cap-rate arbitrage
The barbell is a spread trade. US gateway distress at going-in yields with deep liquidity versus Latin American assets at higher stabilized yields with structural growth. Blended cost of capital is lower than either leg financed alone, and risks are uncorrelated.
03
FX, repatriation, and dollarization
Currency is a return driver. Ecuador's dollarization removes translation risk entirely. Mexico and Colombia require dynamic hedging at the debt and distribution level, and a preference for USD-denominated contracts where possible. Model repatriation from day one.
04
Operating model and brand selection
International brands de-risk ramp-up and anchor the asset to a global reservation system. In thin or first-mover markets like Ecuador, the right local operating partner matters more than the flag. Align incentives so the operator earns on profit, not only revenue.
05
Experiential differentiation as the new luxury
Across the Americas, the premium is migrating from the room to the experience. Design, food and beverage, culture, nature, and authenticity — this is the lens of the experience economy. Capacity-controlled, hard-to-replicate product (from Galápagos to fine-origin destinations) sustains pricing power that commodity supply cannot.

Three probability-weighted paths with explicit allocation implications

Scenario Probability Narrative Allocation Early-Warning Signals
Base 60% Soft landing, selective flows. US value entries clear slowly; LatAm growth steady; rates ease gradually. Balanced barbell; overweight Mexico and US gateway value; phase Colombia and Ecuador. Hotel maturity extensions vs. resolutions; Mexican arrivals trend; LatAm pipeline absorption.
Bull 25% Repricing accelerates. Rate relief plus refinancing distress create motivated sellers; LatAm absorption outruns supply. Lean into US distressed acquisition; extend duration; scale Mexico and Colombia. Faster rate cuts; spread compression in hotel debt; rising transaction volume in gateways.
Bear 15% Risk-off and political shock. Capital freezes; LatAm security or regulatory shocks widen spreads. Flight to quality: USD-natural Ecuador, liquid US gateway, defensive cash; pause peso and COP duration. Country-risk spikes (Ecuador, Colombia); MXN/COP depreciation; stalled financings.

In every path, the markets whose only thesis was the event itself are the ones that underperform. The base case carries the portfolio; the bull case rewards conviction on the US distressed leg; the bear case rewards the USD-natural and liquid positions.
